# Placing fittings and equipment: the app against Revit (checked 2026-10-24)

Checked in the code (`model_edit.rs::family_click`, `model_view.rs` Family tool, `crates/route`, `crates/document/src/layout.rs`).

## What the app does today
- **Fittings made by routing** (`crates/route`): elbows, transitions, tees and taps are made while a duct or pipe is drawn or a layout is finished, from the route's own part catalogue (`route/src/catalogue.rs`, `type_name` and `part_code`). They are placed and turned by the geometry of the run, as Revit does when you draw.
- **Family fittings (the new Pipe elbow, reducer, tee) and equipment**: placed by one click in a plan view, snapped to a 50 mm grid, turned by the angle typed in the options bar, at the elevation typed. Nothing else.

## What Revit does that the app does not
| Revit behaviour | App |
|---|---|
| A fitting or piece of equipment snaps to the open connector of a pipe/duct/equipment near the cursor, and turns to face it | No. Placement ignores connectors |
| Spacebar turns an element while placing it | Not for families (only walls flip) |
| Dropping a tee on a pipe splits the pipe and connects all three ends | No |
| Dropping an elbow or a transition in line cuts the run there | No |
| Moving equipment drags the connected pipes and ducts | No (the link is kept, the runs are not moved) |
| Routing Preferences choose the fitting family and type by size | The route catalogue chooses its own parts; the family fittings are not wired to it |
| Place on Face / on Work Plane (ceiling terminals, wall devices) | No (also in gap analysis) |
| Preview of the element before the click | Done (step 1) |

## Plan (smallest steps first)
1. DONE (apps/desktop/src/snap_place.rs) - Connector snap on placement: a click of the Family tool within 0.4 m of an open duct/pipe end or equipment connector places the family on it, turned to face it, at its height, with the table type of that size, joined, as one undo step; Spacebar turns by 90 degrees, Tab takes the next connector. Also done: a ghost preview before the click (in plan and in the 3D view: the body outline where the click would put it, joined connectors ringed, a short label; red when the cut is refused), and a join between two placed families (`FamLink` in the MEP data, saved as `K|` records; a connector is in one join; moving only one of two joined families takes them apart with a note, moving both keeps them). Original text: with the Family tool, a ghost of the family follows the cursor; when an open connector of a run or equipment is within a pick distance, the family turns so that its first compatible connector faces it, moves so the two connectors meet, takes the size of the one it meets (if the type table has that size), and joins it. Spacebar turns it by 90 degrees; Tab picks the next compatible connector.
2. DONE (snap_place.rs `splice`, document `splice_into_run`) - Splice on a run: a click on the middle of a horizontal duct or pipe with a family that has a straight-through pair of connectors (tee, reducer, valve) cuts the run, puts the family in the gap turned along the run, with the table type of the run's size, and joins both pieces (the old end's joins, end point and taps move to the new piece); one undo step. An elbow is not cut in (it bends the run): it is placed where clicked. Sloped runs are done too (a level element stands at the height of the run under its middle, within 60 mm of the line at both cuts; the two pieces take the slope that joins them to it). Vertical runs do not exist in the model (a run is straight in plan at one height with a slope), so there is nothing to cut; they need a model change first. Not done: taps in the gap (refused), reducers keep the large end on the side of the run's own size (Tab turns it), a table type cannot be chosen before the click (the options bar lists the library's types only). Original text: dropping a two-connector fitting (elbow, reducer) or a tee on the middle of a duct or pipe cuts the run, joins both parts to the fitting and sizes it.
3. DONE (document `transform_commands_noted`, `follow_commands`) - Move with connections: moving, turning or mirroring equipment carries the end of each duct or pipe joined to it (the other end stays; both heights are kept, so the slope follows). A run that would be left with a fitting at its other end at the wrong angle, a branch on it, or a length under 20 mm stays where it was and is unjoined from the equipment, and the message says which. The elbow at the far end of a carried pipe is rebuilt for the new angle (corner kept, same bend radius factor, both runs re-cut to its ports, links keep their ports); if that cannot be done (a run under 20 mm, a branch on the next run, no elbow fits) the pipe is unjoined with a note. Revit's own help pages do not say in words that the far fitting follows; this follows what the Revit user community describes. Move has a Disjoin check box in its options bar (Revit's option): the elements move alone, every join to what stays is broken first and the note says so; with it a joined pipe can be moved on its own, without it that is still refused. Not done: following in 3D heights. Original text: moving equipment or a fitting stretches the runs joined to it.
4. Routing preferences that point at family types by size, so Generate Layout and drawing a run use the table the user maintains.
